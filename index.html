StyleJudge — Response Formality as a Driver of Differential Judicial Rigor in LLM-as-a-Judge Systems
AI Safety Researcher  ·  Electric Sheep · FutureKind Winter Fellowship  ·  Mentor: Philip Kratz (University of Santiago de Compostela)
Nov 2025 – Present
Empirical investigation into StyleBias — the counter-intuitive finding that high structural formality increases judicial rigor in LLM-as-a-Judge systems rather than conferring leniency, invalidating the construct validity of current RLHF evaluation infrastructure. Catalysed by MANTA observations where Claude's structured responses received systematically harsher scores than GPT's, overriding self-preference bias.
  • Discovered the Scrutiny Effect: structured responses trigger harsher evaluation than casual equivalents — GPT-4o scored higher than Claude even when Claude 3.5 Sonnet was the judge, directly refuting the self-enhancement narrative.
  • Developed StyleJudge-Bench: controlled benchmark across 4 formality levels × 4 judge models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3.1 70B) × 3 domains; defined 4 novel metrics including StyleBias Score (SBS) and False Penalty Rate (FPR).
  • Demonstrated an adversarial RLHF reward hacking pathway: models can maximise reward signal by degrading output structure to trigger judicial leniency rather than improving reasoning — with direct implications for training-time safety and benchmark integrity.
